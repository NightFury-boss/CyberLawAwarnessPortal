# Plain-Language Content Standard for Learner-Facing Copy

This rule establishes the voice, tone, vocabulary, and clarity standard for all learner-facing and public-facing interfaces in the Cyber Law Awareness Portal.

## Core Principle
**Keep the underlying system sophisticated; make the user experience simple.**
An ordinary student, school teacher, parent, or first-time digital citizen should be able to navigate, learn, and take action without needing technical, academic, psychometric, or legal jargon.

---

## The Seven Writing Principles

1. **Start with the reader's question or need:**
   - Lead with immediate relevance: what happened, what action to take next, or what practical skill is being taught.
   - Avoid bureaucratic or academic framing ("This module assesses behavioral resilience parameters").
   - Prefer direct benefit ("Learn how to spot fake payment messages before clicking").

2. **Use familiar language before specialist terminology:**
   - Prefer common, conversational words over abstract nominalizations.
   - Use active voice and short, focused sentences.
   - Standard portal terminology mappings:
     - *Behavioral Evolution* &rarr; *How Your Decisions Change*
     - *Learning Interventions* &rarr; *What You've Practiced*
     - *Knowledge Practice* &rarr; *What You've Learned*
     - *Delayed Recall Practice* &rarr; *Practice Again Later*
     - *Emerging Gap* &rarr; *Something to Practice*
     - *Verification Behaviour* &rarr; *Checking Before Trusting*
     - *Defensive Portfolio* &rarr; *Your Learning Record*
     - *Statutory Knowledge Result* &rarr; *Law Knowledge Check*
     - *Baseline Assessment* &rarr; *First Check*
     - *Final Assessment* &rarr; *Final Check*
     - *Workspace* &rarr; *Dashboard*

3. **Explain difficult ideas with practical examples:**
   - Anchor legal rules and security advice in everyday situations (e.g. paying for groceries via UPI, receiving an SMS about electricity disconnection, receiving an unexpected APK attachment).
   - Use plain-language analogies and parenthetical notes alongside statutory provisions.

4. **Keep legal references precise:**
   - Never alter official statute titles, section numbers, or legal definitions where statutory precision is required.
   - Clearly identify:
     - Active statutory provisions (e.g., IT Act 2000 provisions in force, BNS 2023 § 318/319).
     - Repealed or unconstitutional provisions (e.g., Section 66A struck down in *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India*).
     - Provisions whose operational commencement is pending notification (e.g., DPDP Act 2023 rules).
   - For financial fraud and cyber emergencies, state clearly: **Call National Cybercrime Helpline 1930** and visit **cybercrime.gov.in** within the 2–3 hour Golden Hour for CFCFRMS inter-bank hold coordination, without guaranteeing fund freeze or recovery.

5. **Never claim more than the evidence supports:**
   - Distinguish a knowledge check (quiz) from a simulated decision (scenario) and from retention over time (spaced reviews).
   - Do not claim a quiz score proves real-world immunity or behavioral mastery.
   - Do not claim completing a lesson guarantees safety from all cyber threats.
   - Never imply educational awareness constitutes formal legal advice or representation.

6. **Keep internal engineering terminology out of learner-facing copy unless genuinely necessary:**
   - Never expose internal database fields, backend collection names, or scoring algorithms to the learner.
   - Map the six canonical dimensions to plain-language labels across all learner views:
     - `TR` &rarr; *Spotting Dangers*
     - `SI` &rarr; *Spotting Fake Senders*
     - `VB` &rarr; *Checking Before Trusting*
     - `DQ` &rarr; *Making Thoughtful Choices*
     - `FP` &rarr; *Staying Calm with Safe Messages*
     - `UA` &rarr; *Not Clicking Without Checking*
   - Keep error messages, empty states, and button labels reassuring, respectful, and actionable.

7. **Use consistent names for the same concept across the portal:**
   - Avoid referring to the same concept by different names in different views (e.g., do not alternate between "Diagnostic", "Baseline", "Initial Check", and "Pre-Test"; use **First Check** consistently in student-facing views).
   - Maintain naming continuity across navigation breadcrumbs, sidebar items, dashboard widgets, and modals.
